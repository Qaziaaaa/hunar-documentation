# 01 — inDrive Research

## Why inDrive matters to this project

inDrive is the strongest reference for the **negotiated-price marketplace concept**. Its broader model is built around giving users and service providers more direct choice over agreements rather than relying only on centrally fixed pricing.

inDrive says its bid-based pricing model began in 2012 and has expanded beyond rides into city/intercity transport, courier, freight and other urban services, including housekeeping and small home repairs. [Source: inDrive Blog](https://blog.indrive.com/ne-np/article/indrive-celebrating-5-billion-deals-and-counting)

A 2025 interview with inDrive's SVP of New Verticals describes its peer-to-peer price bidding model as a way for users and service providers to agree on a fair price directly. [Source: Intelligent CIO](https://www.intelligentcio.com/latam/2025/07/02/qa-andries-smit-svp-of-new-verticals-indrive/)

## Customer-side model

Core pattern:

1. Customer describes the request.
2. Customer proposes or expects a price.
3. Providers can respond/accept according to the marketplace model.
4. Customer has choice rather than being forced into one provider.
5. Agreement is made before the service/ride.

For the home-services product, the important transferable concept is **provider choice + negotiated visit/service price**.

## Worker/provider-side model

The inDrive model gives providers more control over the work they accept. The current Pakistan app description says drivers/delivery partners can see job/trip details and choose the work that fits their schedule. [Source: Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=sinet.startup.inDriver)

## Pricing lesson

### What is attractive

- Negotiation can reduce perceived overcharging.
- Customer gets choice.
- Worker can reject jobs that are not worth the travel/time.
- Pricing can adapt to difficult or unusual jobs.

### Risk for home services

A repair job is different from a ride. A customer may not know the true cost before inspection. Therefore, copying a pure one-price bid model for the **entire repair** can create disputes.

### Recommended interpretation

Use negotiation mainly for the **visit/inspection charge** before the worker comes.

After inspection, require a separate repair estimate and explicit customer approval before chargeable repair work begins.

This is a product recommendation based on the difference between predictable transport fares and uncertain repair scope; it is not a claim about inDrive's own home-repair implementation.

## Pakistan relevance

inDrive has a strong Pakistan footprint. Its Pakistan app listing currently shows multiple Pakistani cities and its 2026 product expansion includes grocery delivery. [Source: Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=sinet.startup.inDriver) [Source: inDrive Blog](https://blog.indrive.com/article/indrive-launches-grocery-delivery-with-krave-mart)

This matters because the team can learn from a model that is already familiar to Pakistani users.

## What to copy

- User/provider choice
- Transparent offer comparison
- Negotiated price where uncertainty exists
- Provider acceptance/rejection of work
- Clear provider profile signals

## What to improve

For home services, add:

- Separate visit charge vs repair price
- Price lock after customer approval
- Evidence-based repair estimate
- Scope-change approval
- Stronger job-completion evidence
- Better dispute trail

## What not to copy blindly

- Treating every home-service job like a simple ride
- Allowing ambiguous verbal price changes
- Making price the only provider-selection factor
- Building a giant super-app before the core service marketplace is proven
